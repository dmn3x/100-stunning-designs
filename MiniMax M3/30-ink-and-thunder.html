Ink & Thunder

a poet's notebook · 23 March

There is a small storm in the kitchen each morning. It waits until the kettle has decided to sing. Then the cloud, honest as a postcard, opens its mouth.

Rain has been reading the headlines. It tells me: the river turned under the bridge. The swallow returned. The locksmith has left town.

I sit with the page between us. Ink becomes storm. Storm becomes ink. We argue politely, and the door stays open for the kind visitor.

Tonight the cat refuses to come in. She is reading the lightning as if it were a letter she had been waiting for.

My notebook is a gentle conspiracy against exact weather. I sign it loosely; the storm signs in capitals.

By 23:14 the rain has stopped. The cloud, slightly embarrassed, retreats over the linden. I pour a second cup, and write a polite reply.

It is now confirmed: the storm and I share a postal code. We are neighbours. We are quiet. We are reading each other aloud.

The draft under the door is the storm's small visiting card. Each line is a paragraph; the cat is the editor.

There is a poem tonight that is also a forecast. I will set it in italic so the cloud knows I am not unkind.

— signed — E.M., with an inkwell that trembles politely.

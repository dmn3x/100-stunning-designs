Issue №7 is dedicated to the lemon, a fruit that has ruined more dishes than it has rescued and remained everyone's favourite anyway.

Inside: a recipe for lemon-butter pasta that took five tests, an essay on sourness as a moral category, and a fold-out wheel of citrus seasons.

Lemon-butter pasta, for one

80g spaghetti · zest of one lemon
30g unsalted butter · a small handful of parmesan
cracked black pepper · a single basil leaf

Cook the pasta. Melt the butter. Toss. Eat warm, sitting down.

From the editor

Citrus is not a flavour. It is a temperament — sharp, honest, unforgiving of heavy-handed cooks. A pinch of zest will rescue a dull sauce; a squeeze too many will erase the dish entirely.

This issue collects forty-one notes on lemons, limes, bergamots, and one forgotten shaddock. Read slowly. Fold the corners if you wish.
